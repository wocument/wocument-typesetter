# Closing page

*Rule:* H7. Closing page: one picture, a short text, an end mark. *Built by:* component-builder. *Status:* Approved. The owner asked that the content decides how it applies (2026-11-24).

## What it is

The last editorial page of the issue should be quiet: one picture, a short text of two or three paragraphs, and an end mark. It tells the reader "this is the end", like a full stop at the end of a sentence.

On phones the picture comes first, then the text, and the end mark closes the text.

## Example

A closing page called "Final touch": one photo of a handmade lamp and three short paragraphs about the person who made it, ending with ●.

## The parts and how they relate

- **The parts:** a section label (for example "Final touch"), a title, 2 to 3 paragraphs, an end mark and one picture.
- **Limits:** no pull quotes and no sidebars. It is a coda, a quiet ending.
- **How it changes with the publication's style:** restrained publications use a small picture inside the margins. Expressive ones use a full-bleed picture with the title over it.
- **The end mark:** uses the house shape (see D5).

## Decide from the content

Use a closing page only when the edition has a short, quiet piece that suits an ending: an object, a memory, a place, a final thought. Its picture is chosen for calm, not impact. The dial sets the treatment: restrained publications use a small picture inside the margins; expressive ones may run the picture full bleed with the title over it. When the edition has no such piece, end on the last story's end mark instead; never invent a closing text.

## On each screen

- Laptops and wide screens (1024 px and up): Picture in one half, text in the other.
- Tablets (640 to 1023 px): The picture on top.
- Phones (under 640 px): Picture, then text, then the end mark.
