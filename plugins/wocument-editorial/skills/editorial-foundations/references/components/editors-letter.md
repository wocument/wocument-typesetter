# Editor's letter

*Rule:* H3. Editor's letter: the design follows the letter. *Built by:* component-builder. *Status:* Changed by the owner (2026-11-24).

## What it is

The editor's letter is a short personal note from the editor at the front of the issue. It ends with the editor's "sign-off": their signature, name and job title, and sometimes a small portrait.

The sign-off is the last thing the reader reads, so it goes where the reading ends. In print that is the foot of the page. On a wide screen with two columns, the reading ends at the foot of the last column, usually the bottom right. On a phone, it is simply after the last paragraph.

A portrait of the editor can also open the letter, at the top. What it must never do is float somewhere in the middle, or sit at the top as a sign-off before the letter has even started.

**Owner's change (2026-11-24).** The editor's letter has no single correct design. Its design is chosen from the letter itself and from the publication's profile. The one thing that holds in every design is the reading path: the letter reads in one clear order, and the sign-off (signature, name and title) comes where the reading ends, never above the letter or floating in the middle.

## Example

On a laptop: the letter runs in two columns, and at the foot of the right-hand column comes the handwritten signature, then "Maya Chen, Editor-in-chief", with a small portrait. On an iPhone: one column, and the signature comes after the last paragraph.

## The parts and how they relate

- **The parts:** a section label, a title (a line taken from the letter, not the words "Editor's letter"), 3 to 6 short paragraphs, the sign-off (signature, name, title), and an optional portrait.
- **Where things go:** follow the reading order. The sign-off is the last thing read, so at every screen size it goes where the reading ends.
- **How it changes with the publication's style:** restrained publications use no portrait and a small signature. Expressive ones open the page with a large portrait and set the title big.
- **Optional preview:** small numbered pictures from the issue may run beside the letter as a preview, with captions matched by number.
- **Never:** put the sign-off above the letter or in a sidebar.

## Decide from the content

Read the letter's brief first, then choose. **A short, warm, personal letter** (warmth 4 or 5, a lot of "I"): one column, a portrait of the editor at the top or beside the sign-off, a handwritten signature. **A longer letter that argues a point** (seriousness and formality 4 or 5): two columns on wide screens, no portrait or a small one, a title taken from the strongest line, the sign-off at the foot of the last column. **A letter that introduces a themed edition**: small numbered pictures of the edition's stories beside the letter, with captions matched by number. **A restrained profile** uses a small signature and no portrait; **an expressive one** may open with a large portrait and a big title. If the editor supplied no portrait or signature, use a typed sign-off in the second voice; never invent either.

## On each screen

- Laptops and wide screens (1024 px and up): 2 columns; the sign-off and portrait at the foot of the right column.
- Tablets (640 to 1023 px): 1 column; the sign-off after the text.
- Phones (under 640 px): 1 column; a small portrait at the top or beside the sign-off.
