# Photo essay

*Rule:* H6. Photo essay: one picture per screen, one line each. *Built by:* component-builder. *Status:* Approved.

## What it is

A photo essay tells a story mainly through pictures. Each picture gets a whole screen to itself, with at most one line of text (a caption), so the reader moves through the story picture by picture.

On wide screens two pictures may sit side by side as a pair. On phones each picture is full width with its line of text directly underneath.

## Example

"A year on the coast": 8 photos from January to December, one per screen, each with a single line such as "March. The first boats go out."

## The parts and how they relate

- **The parts:** an opening (title and one sentence), 4 to 12 pictures, one line of text per picture, and the credits at the end.
- **Order:** the pictures follow a sequence (light, place, season, time of day), not a catalogue. The image skill decides it.
- **Limits:** no paragraphs of body text between the pictures. A single pull quote may take the place of one picture.
- **How it changes with the publication's style:** restrained publications keep the pictures inside the margins on paper colour. Expressive ones run them full bleed on a dark background.
- **On a phone:** one picture per screen-height block, with its line directly under it.

## Decide from the content

Use a photo essay only when the brief's visual pull is 5 and there are at least 4 strong pictures that form a sequence. If the pictures are fewer or weaker, use a picture-led story with breathers instead (E5). The order of the pictures and the one line under each come from the content: the line says what the reader cannot see, not what they can.

## On each screen

- Laptops and wide screens (1024 px and up): One or two pictures per screen.
- Tablets (640 to 1023 px): One picture per screen.
- Phones (under 640 px): Full-width pictures, with a line of text under each.
