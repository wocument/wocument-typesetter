# Editorial art direction

> **Placeholder, in training.** This skill is built while the study builds are reviewed (method 14). Every review round adds what it taught under "Lessons" below, and the rules it relies on go into `editorial-foundations`. Until the full workflow is written, follow the card and the lessons.

## Card

| | |
|---|---|
| **Is** | The editorial decisions, each with a reason tied to the brief and the foundations. |
| **Is not** | Choosing or cropping pictures (image-art-direction), writing the `.woct` or `.woc`, building components, inventing a theme (it chooses from the theme catalogue, or asks publication-system). |
| **Prerequisites** | `design/content-brief.md`, the theme catalogue (`editorial-foundations/references/theme-catalogue.md` format), the foundations. |
| **Inputs** | The brief, the catalogue, optional references or a mood board. |
| **Output** | `design/direction.md`: archetype, theme and why, grid archetype, visual language in words, a pacing map scene by scene (with each scene's screen target), the chosen anchors and visuals, components needed, what must stay true on phone, tablet and wide. |
| **Answers** | What should this feel like? Which theme and grid? How is the reading paced? Which moments become visual? |
| **Gate** | The orchestrator stops here for the owner's approval. |

## Lessons from review rounds

**Round 1, style-issue study build (2026-11-26)**

- **Every scene has a screen target** in the pacing map: 1, 2 or 3 whole screens, or an equal share of one screen (1/2, 1/3, 1/4) with its neighbours. A page meant to be seen at once (editor's letter, contributors, an opener, a list spread) is one screen. Never plan 1.5. (J1)
- **Text size is never the fitting lever.** Fit with pictures, space and margins only. If the content cannot fit its target without changing text size, change the target (two whole screens) or the scene's composition. (J1)
- **"Scene break" in a brief is a boundary**, not a scene. Never plan a scene that holds only a rule or a gap. (J2)
- **Chrome is not content.** The issue name and the section label sit in the head strip (folio), never as frames in the text columns. The byline belongs with the heading block or the end of the text. (J5)
- **Plan the hierarchy as roles with steps**: headline to deck about 3 to 5 times, deck to body 1.2 to 1.4, labels 0.7 to 0.85 set apart by weight or case. Headline and deck share an axis. (J6)
- **Pull quotes**: at least one quote in a long feature spans two columns inside the prose (`place=float, size=wide`); one-column quotes alone read as captions.
- **Cut what does not read on screen.** A print composition that only works on paper (the gathered "look" strip with a row of labels) is dropped, not imitated.
- **Read a page fraction against the page, not the spread.** A desktop screen holds a whole spread. (J3, with image-art-direction)
